## 6. Pitfalls and phones

This skill lists the mistakes real teams make with push, and the parts that are outside your control.

### Seven mistakes

1. **Using notification messages and expecting your code to run.** Symptom: it works in the foreground, but in the background a plain banner appears with none of your logic. Fix: send data messages and build the notification yourself.
2. **Never deleting dead tokens.** Symptom: sends slow down, logs fill with `UNREGISTERED`, and some users get duplicates (old and new install). Fix: delete on that error, and drop tokens on logout.
3. **Treating FCM as guaranteed.** Symptom: "I never got it" reports with no trace. Fix: keep the truth in your backend (an inbox or unseen list the app also reads on open). Push only prompts a look.
4. **Committing the service-account key.** Symptom: a leaked key lets anyone send pushes as you. Fix: keep it out of git, load it from an environment variable or a root-only file, and rotate it if exposed.
5. **A long TTL on time-sensitive messages.** Symptom: a "ring" arrives hours later when the phone reconnects. Fix: set TTL to what the message is worth, which is minutes.
6. **Forgetting the notification permission and token refresh.** Symptom: new Android users get nothing, and tokens in your table go stale silently. Fix: ask for `POST_NOTIFICATIONS` at a sensible moment, and re-upload the token whenever the SDK reports a new one and on each login.
7. **Stuffing private data or large blobs into the payload.** Symptom: you hit the size cap, or leak content to a third party. Fix: send ids and fetch details over your own API.

### The phone is the hidden tax

- Aggressive battery managers on some phone brands delay or drop pushes for apps that are not whitelisted. You cannot fix this from the server. [consensus]
- Doze and priority rules mean "high priority" is a privilege. Abuse gets you throttled.
- The community-maintained site dontkillmyapp.com lists maker-by-maker behaviour. Use it when "it works on my phone" meets "it never arrives on theirs".

### Calls and always-on audio

- Push only **wakes** the app. For a live walkie-talkie, the push says "wake up and reconnect", and a socket or WebRTC carries the audio.
- Ringing via a high-priority push is the standard pattern, but Android adds rules: a full-screen-intent permission, foreground service types, and limits on starting the microphone from the background. These tighten with each Android version. [fast-moving, verify on your target versions]
- In practice the user usually taps to answer, the app comes to the front, and only then does it start capturing the microphone.
- Battery cost matters too. A held-open connection is cheap when idle, but the figures depend on the phone and network, so measure on your own devices.
