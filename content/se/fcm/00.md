## How to read this track

This track gives you judgment about Firebase, and real depth on one part of it: Firebase Cloud Messaging (FCM), the free pipe that lets your own server wake a closed phone. It follows the learning guide `learning-guides/firebase.md`.

The running example is LevelUp's party nudges. Today the Android app checks the server every 15 minutes for a nudge. A push would deliver it in seconds, and would be the first step towards ringing a walkie-talkie call.

### The big picture in five lines

1. **Firebase** is Google's catalogue of app services (auth, databases, hosting, analytics, push). You can adopt one and ignore the rest.
2. **FCM** is the push piece. Your server hands Google a message addressed to one app install, and Google delivers it over the phone's shared connection.
3. FCM only **transports**. Your accounts, rules and data stay in your own backend, and users never log in with Google.
4. Delivery is **best-effort**. Keep the truth in your database; a push only says "look now".
5. Adopt the leaf (push), and think hard before adopting the roots (Firebase's database and login).

### Tags you will see

- **[consensus]** widely agreed
- **[depends]** contested, or depends on context
- **[fast-moving]** changes quickly, so verify before relying on it
- **[unsure]** not confident

### Order

Start with why push exists, then the Firebase map and tokens (either order), then sending, then the two judgment skills (use it or not, pitfalls), and finish with the review.
