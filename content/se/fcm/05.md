## 5. Use it, or not?

This skill is the decision: when FCM is the right tool, when it is not, and what else you could use.

### Use FCM when

- You need to reach a user whose app is closed, within seconds.
- Your backend already exists, and you only want the delivery pipe.
- Android is your main platform. FCM is the native route, and Play Services is the carrier.
- Message volume is small to moderate, and the content is not highly sensitive.
- You can accept best-effort delivery.

### Do not use FCM (or not alone) when

- Users are on phones without Google Play Services (some Chinese-market devices, de-Googled ROMs). You need another route.
- Delivery must be guaranteed or auditable (payments, safety alerts). Use SMS, or an in-app inbox as the source of truth, with push only as the "go look" signal.
- The message body is private and you do not want it passing through Google. Send "you have a message" and fetch the content over your own connection.
- You want a live audio stream or live state. Push only *wakes* the app; the live part needs a socket or WebRTC after it wakes.
- Your whole audience is on the web, and a browser notification is enough (see Web Push below).

### Alternatives

| Option | Best when | Breaks when | Cost and complexity |
|---|---|---|---|
| Polling (the simple approach) | Seconds do not matter; few users | You want sub-minute latency; Android caps background work at about 15 minutes | Free, trivial |
| FCM | Native Android app, own backend, wake the app | No Play Services; guaranteed delivery needed | Free pipe; Firebase project, SDK and key handling |
| Web Push (VAPID, a browser standard) | Web or PWA users, no Google account | Browser closed or suspended; cannot show a full-screen call UI | Free; you manage keys and subscriptions |
| Own foreground service and socket | Walkie-talkie style always-on | Battery, OS kills, maker battery savers | Free; the most native code and testing |
| Self-hosted push (UnifiedPush, ntfy) | No-Google, privacy-first audiences | Users must install a distributor app | Free; you run a server |
| Hosted wrapper (OneSignal and similar) | Marketing pushes, dashboards, segments | You want control or zero third-party data | Free tier then paid [fast-moving]; still rides FCM on Android |

For LevelUp, polling is what exists today. FCM is the upgrade that makes nudges instant, and it is the natural wake-up for a future walkie-talkie.
