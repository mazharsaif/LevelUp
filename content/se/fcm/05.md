## 5. Use it, or not?

This skill is the decision: when Firebase is the right tool, when it is not, and what else you could use.

### Use Firebase when

- You are a small team or one person and need auth, data and push this week, with no backend to run.
- The data is document-shaped and driven by the app (profiles, notes, chats, to-do lists), with few cross-document queries.
- You want real-time sync or offline support almost for free.
- Your users are mostly on mobile, where the SDKs are strongest.
- You want a free crash reporter and analytics source for a published app.

### Do not use Firebase (or not that part) when

- Your data is relational and you need joins, multi-row transactions or ad-hoc SQL reporting. Firestore will fight you; use Postgres.
- You already run a backend and database that work. Adding Firestore creates two sources of truth. Take only the leaves (push, Crashlytics, Hosting).
- You cannot accept vendor lock-in on the data layer, or you must self-host or meet a data-residency rule you cannot satisfy.
- Your read patterns are large and unpredictable, so per-operation billing is a risk you cannot cap.
- You need heavy server-side compute or ML serving. Functions are for glue.

### Alternatives

| Option | Best when | Breaks when | Cost and complexity |
|---|---|---|---|
| Own backend (FastAPI and Postgres) | You want control, SQL and portability | You are one person who must also run it | Your time; hosting is small and predictable |
| Firebase | Small team, mobile app, real-time or offline | Relational data, unpredictable reads, must stay portable | Free to start; database billed per operation; high lock-in |
| Supabase (Postgres, auth, storage, open source) | You want Firebase's speed but SQL and an exit | You need Firebase's mobile offline maturity | Hosted tiers or self-host; lower lock-in [depends] |
| Appwrite or PocketBase (self-hosted) | You want to own the box | You cannot operate a server | Free software; you run and patch it |
| A cloud provider's mobile backend (such as AWS Amplify) | You are already deep in that cloud | You want Firebase's simple SDKs | Powerful, with more moving parts |
| Keep your backend, add only FCM | A working backend that needs push | You wanted a whole backend replaced | Near zero; the lowest lock-in |

### Worked decision: LevelUp

LevelUp already has a FastAPI backend, its own accounts, progress stored per learner, and a tested merge for offline edits.

- **Authentication and Firestore** would duplicate the accounts and the progress store and create a second source of truth. Skip them.
- **Hosting** is already covered by Caddy.
- **FCM** is the one thing the app lacks: nudges wait for a 15-minute poll today.

Verdict: adopt **only FCM**, keep the user-to-token table in the existing backend, and revisit Crashlytics when there is a published app. It is the cheapest, lowest-lock-in answer.
