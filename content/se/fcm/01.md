## 1. Why push exists

This skill covers the problem that push notifications solve and the mental model behind them. If you only remember this skill, you should still not misuse FCM.

### The pain

You ship a small app with your own login and your own FastAPI backend. One feature needs to tell a user something *now*: a friend nudged you, a long job finished, a model drifted. The phone is in a pocket and the app is closed.

First attempt: the app polls `GET /api/nudges` every few minutes. It works, but Android will not run a background job more often than about every 15 minutes, so "now" means "within a quarter of an hour".

### Why polling and sockets fall short

| Approach | What goes wrong |
|---|---|
| Poll on a timer | Android limits background work to roughly every 15 minutes, and each poll wastes battery when there is nothing new |
| Keep your own socket open from a background service | The OS, or the phone maker's battery saver, kills it, and every app doing this drains the battery |
| Ask the user to open the app | Defeats the point of a nudge |

### The shared connection

Phones solved this by running **one** shared, OS-managed connection to a push gateway, and letting every app ride it. On Android, Google's gateway is **Firebase Cloud Messaging (FCM)**. It is one product inside **Firebase**, Google's bundle of app-backend services.

```
 Your server (FastAPI)            Google                  Phone
 ────────────────────     ────────────────────     ───────────────────
 "user bob → token T9"──► FCM gateway          ──► Play Services (one shared
  POST messages:send       (stores it, retries)      always-on connection)
                                                          │
                                                          ▼
                                                    your app wakes for a few seconds
                                                    → shows a notification / rings
```

### What FCM does and does not do

- It **transports** a small message from your server to one app install.
- It does **not** know your users, store your data, or guarantee delivery. Design as if a message can arrive late, or not at all.
- Think of it as a mailbox service with a courier you do not control.
