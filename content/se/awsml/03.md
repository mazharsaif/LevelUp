## 3. GenAI and foundation models

This skill covers AIF-C01 Domain 2 (24%): what foundation models are, how they generate text, where they fail, and the AWS services for building with them.

### Foundation models and LLMs

A **foundation model (FM)** is a large model pre-trained on huge amounts of general data, which can then be adapted to many tasks. A **large language model (LLM)** is a foundation model for text. Most modern LLMs use the **transformer** architecture, whose **attention** mechanism lets each word weigh every other word in the input.

Other FM types: image generation models (diffusion models), multimodal models that read images and text, and embedding models that turn content into vectors.

### Tokens, context and embeddings

- A **token** is a chunk of text, roughly three-quarters of an English word on average. Models read and write tokens, and you pay per token.
- The **context window** is the maximum number of tokens (prompt plus answer) the model can handle at once.
- An **embedding** is a list of numbers (a vector) that represents meaning. Similar texts get vectors that are close together, measured with **cosine similarity**. Embeddings power semantic search and RAG.

### Controlling the output

| Setting | Effect |
|---|---|
| Temperature | Low (0–0.3) = focused, repeatable; high (0.8–1) = varied, creative |
| Top-p | Sample only from the smallest set of tokens whose probabilities add up to p |
| Top-k | Sample only from the k most likely tokens |
| Max tokens | Hard limit on answer length (and cost) |
| Stop sequences | Text that ends generation |

For extraction, classification and anything that must be consistent, use a low temperature.

### Capabilities and limits

GenAI is good at drafting, summarising, translating, classifying text, extracting fields, answering questions over documents and writing code. Its limits:

- **Hallucination**: confident output that is factually wrong. Reduce it with RAG, low temperature, grounding checks and asking for citations.
- **Non-determinism**: the same prompt can give different answers.
- **Knowledge cut-off**: the model does not know recent events unless you supply them.
- **Cost and latency** grow with model size and token count.
- **Interpretability**: it is hard to explain why a specific answer came out.

Business metrics to judge a GenAI app include accuracy or task success, user satisfaction, conversion, cost per interaction and time saved.

### AWS services for GenAI

- **Amazon Bedrock**: a fully managed, serverless API to foundation models from Amazon (Nova, Titan), Anthropic, Meta, Mistral and others. It adds knowledge bases (RAG), agents, Guardrails, model evaluation, fine-tuning and prompt management. Your data is not used to train the base models.
- **Amazon SageMaker JumpStart**: a hub of open models you deploy onto your own SageMaker endpoints, when you need full control of hosting.
- **Amazon Q**: ready-made generative AI assistants for business and developers.
- **PartyRock**: a free playground for building small Bedrock apps without code.

Pricing options in Bedrock: **on-demand** (pay per token), **batch inference** (about half the on-demand price for offline jobs), and **provisioned throughput** (reserved model units for steady, high or custom-model traffic).

### Lab: first Bedrock calls (about $0.01)

1. In the Bedrock console (eu-central-1), open Model access and check that Amazon Nova models are available.
2. Call Nova Micro from the CLI:

```bash
aws bedrock-runtime converse --model-id eu.amazon.nova-micro-v1:0 \
  --messages '[{"role":"user","content":[{"text":"Explain overfitting in two sentences."}]}]' \
  --inference-config '{"maxTokens":150,"temperature":0.2}' --profile personal --region eu-central-1
```

3. Run it three times at temperature 0.2 and three times at 1.0, and compare how much the answers change. Read `usage.inputTokens` and `outputTokens`, and work out the cost from the Bedrock pricing page.
4. Embed five sentences (two about dogs, two about tax, one other) with `amazon.titan-embed-text-v2:0` using `aws bedrock-runtime invoke-model`. In Python, print the cosine-similarity matrix and check that same-topic pairs score highest.

### Self-check

1. Which setting makes output more deterministic?
2. What is an embedding used for?
3. A chatbot invents a refund policy that does not exist. What is this called, and what is the best fix?
4. When would you pick SageMaker JumpStart over Bedrock?
5. Which Bedrock pricing option fits a nightly job summarising 100,000 documents?

**Answers.** 1: Lower temperature (or a lower top-p / top-k). 2: Representing meaning as vectors for semantic search, clustering and RAG retrieval. 3: A hallucination; ground the answers in the real policy documents with RAG and add a grounding check. 4: When you need full control of the model hosting, instance type or an open model that Bedrock does not offer. 5: Batch inference.
