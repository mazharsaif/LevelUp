## How to use this track

This track takes you from zero to two AWS certifications: **AWS Certified AI Practitioner (AIF-C01)** around week 4, then **AWS Certified Machine Learning Engineer – Associate (MLA-C02)** around week 12. It assumes you can write some Python and use a terminal, but no AWS or machine-learning experience.

MLA-C01 stopped being offered in English on 28 September 2026. MLA-C02 replaces it and adds generative AI throughout: vector databases, RAG, Amazon Bedrock knowledge bases, agents, Guardrails and the cost of foundation models. Older courses and blog posts often still teach C01, so check the exam code before you study from them.

### The two exams

| Exam | Level | Questions | Time | Cost | Pass mark |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| AIF-C01 AI Practitioner | Foundational | 65 (50 scored) | 90 min | $100 | 700 / 1000 |
| MLA-C02 ML Engineer – Associate | Associate | 85 in beta, 65 later | 170 min in beta | $75 in beta ($150 later) | 720 / 1000 |

Both are taken at a Pearson VUE test centre or online with a proctor, and both are valid for 3 years. Unanswered questions count as wrong and there is no penalty for guessing, so never leave a question blank.

### The $5 rule

Every lab is designed so the whole track costs less than $5. You write notebooks on your laptop and only start short jobs on AWS. You use serverless endpoints, the smallest Bedrock models and S3 Vectors as the vector store. Skill 1 sets up budget alerts before anything else. Each lab ends with a cleanup step, and skipping it is the only way to get a surprise bill.

### How each skill works

Each skill has three parts. First the ideas the exam tests. Then a lab with its cost. Then a self-check with answers. A skill counts as learned when you can answer the self-check without looking back.
