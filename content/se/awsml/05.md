## 5. Responsible and secure AI

This skill covers AIF-C01 Domains 4 and 5 (14% each): building AI that is fair, transparent and safe, and securing and governing AI systems on AWS.

### Dimensions of responsible AI

| Dimension | Question it asks |
|---|---|
| Fairness | Does the system treat groups of people equitably? |
| Explainability | Can we explain why it made a decision? |
| Privacy and security | Is personal data protected and used properly? |
| Safety | Does it avoid harmful output? |
| Controllability | Can humans steer and override it? |
| Veracity and robustness | Is it accurate, even with unusual inputs? |
| Governance | Are there processes, owners and audits? |
| Transparency | Do users know they are dealing with AI, and its limits? |

**Bias** often comes from the data: under-represented groups, historical bias in labels, or proxies such as a postcode standing in for ethnicity. Mitigations: diverse and balanced data, bias metrics, human review and regular audits.

### AWS tools for responsible AI

- **SageMaker Clarify**: detects bias in data and in model predictions, and explains predictions with SHAP feature importance.
- **Amazon Bedrock Guardrails**: content filters (hate, violence, sexual content, prompt attacks), denied topics, word filters, PII masking or blocking, and contextual grounding checks that catch hallucinations.
- **Amazon Augmented AI (A2I)**: sends low-confidence predictions to human reviewers.
- **SageMaker Model Cards**: document a model's intended use, training data, metrics and limits.
- **AWS AI Service Cards**: AWS's own documentation of its AI services' intended uses and limits.
- **SageMaker Model Monitor**: watches deployed models for drift, including bias drift.

Explainable models (linear models, decision trees) are easier to justify than deep neural networks. In regulated decisions, interpretability can matter more than a few points of accuracy.

### Security: the shared responsibility model

AWS is responsible for security **of** the cloud: the hardware, network and managed-service infrastructure. You are responsible for security **in** the cloud: your data, IAM permissions, encryption settings, network configuration and what your application does with the model's output.

Key controls:

- **IAM** with least privilege: roles for apps and jobs, no shared users.
- **Encryption** at rest with AWS KMS, and in transit with TLS.
- **Network isolation**: VPCs, private subnets and VPC endpoints (AWS PrivateLink) so traffic stays off the internet.
- **Amazon Macie** finds sensitive data such as PII in S3.
- **Logging**: AWS CloudTrail records API calls; Bedrock model invocation logging records prompts and responses.

GenAI-specific threats: prompt injection, data poisoning of training or RAG data, model output that leaks sensitive data, and over-powerful agents.

### Governance and compliance

- **AWS Config**: records resource configurations and checks them against rules.
- **AWS Audit Manager**: collects evidence for audits.
- **AWS Artifact**: downloads AWS compliance reports such as ISO and SOC.
- **AWS Trusted Advisor**: best-practice checks for security and cost.
- **Data governance**: data lineage, retention policies, data residency (keep data in an EU Region for EU rules), and catalogues such as the AWS Glue Data Catalog.

### Lab: a guardrail (about $0.02)

1. In Bedrock → Guardrails, create a guardrail with a denied topic "investment advice", PII masking for email and phone number, and content filters set to high.
2. Test it in the console with five prompts. Two of them should be blocked: one asking for stock tips, and one containing a fake email address that should come back masked.
3. Delete the guardrail when you are done.

### Self-check

1. Who encrypts your training data in S3 under the shared responsibility model?
2. Which tool measures bias in a dataset and explains predictions?
3. Which document describes a model's intended use and limitations?
4. A loan model is accurate but cannot be explained to the regulator. What do you change?
5. Which service finds PII stored in S3 buckets?

**Answers.** 1: You, the customer; AWS provides KMS, but you must turn encryption on and manage access. 2: SageMaker Clarify. 3: A SageMaker Model Card. 4: Use a more interpretable model, or add explanations with Clarify (SHAP), and document it. 5: Amazon Macie.
