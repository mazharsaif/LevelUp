## 9. Choosing and training models

This skill covers MLA-C02 Tasks 2.1 and 2.2: picking the right modelling approach, and training, tuning and customising models on SageMaker AI and Bedrock.

### Choosing the approach

Go from the least effort to the most:

1. **An AWS AI service** (Textract, Comprehend, Rekognition, Transcribe, Translate, Personalize) when the task is common.
2. **A Bedrock foundation model** with prompting, then RAG, then fine-tuning, for language and generative tasks.
3. **A SageMaker built-in algorithm or a JumpStart model** for custom tabular, image or text models.
4. **Your own code** in SageMaker script mode or your own container, for full control.

Trade-offs the exam asks about: interpretability, latency, cost, training time, domain-specific accuracy and operational overhead.

### Choosing a foundation model on Bedrock

Compare models on: task quality (run an evaluation on your own prompts), context window, modalities (text, image, video), latency, price per 1,000 tokens, Region availability and whether the model can be customised. Use the smallest model that passes your evaluation. Amazon Nova Micro and Lite are cheap and fast; larger models reason better.

RAG patterns: basic retrieve-then-generate; hybrid search (keyword plus vector); reranking the retrieved chunks; query rewriting; metadata filtering; graph RAG for connected entities; agentic RAG, where an agent decides when to search.

### Training on SageMaker AI

- A **training job** pulls a container image, reads data channels from S3 (File, Pipe or Fast File mode), trains on the instance type you choose, and writes `model.tar.gz` to S3.
- **Script mode**: bring your own `train.py` to a managed framework container (scikit-learn, PyTorch, TensorFlow, XGBoost, Hugging Face). It reads `SM_CHANNEL_TRAIN` and writes to `SM_MODEL_DIR`.
- **Managed spot training**: up to 90% cheaper; set `use_spot_instances=True`, `max_wait`, and checkpoints to S3 so the job can resume.
- **Distributed training**: data parallel (each GPU gets a slice of the data) for big datasets; model parallel (the model is split across GPUs) for models too big for one GPU. SageMaker HyperPod runs long, resilient training clusters.
- **SageMaker Debugger** spots vanishing gradients, overfitting and poor GPU use while the job runs.

### Hyperparameters and tuning

- **Epochs**: passes over the data. **Batch size**: rows per update. **Learning rate**: step size; too high diverges, too low is slow. **Steps**: updates in total.
- For XGBoost: `max_depth`, `eta` (learning rate), `num_round`, `subsample`, `min_child_weight`, `alpha` and `lambda` (regularisation).
- **Automatic Model Tuning (AMT)** strategies: Bayesian (smart and efficient), random, grid, and Hyperband (stops weak trials early; good for deep learning). Use early stopping and warm start to save money.

### Overfitting, underfitting, forgetting

- Prevent overfitting with regularisation (L1, L2), dropout, early stopping, more data, a simpler model and cross-validation.
- Fix underfitting with more features, a bigger model or longer training.
- **Catastrophic forgetting**: fine-tuning on a narrow dataset erases general skills. Mitigate it by mixing in general data, using a lower learning rate, using parameter-efficient methods such as LoRA adapters, and evaluating on general tasks too.
- **Ensembles** (bagging, boosting, stacking, voting) combine models for accuracy. A small model in front of a large one, which routes only hard cases onward, cuts cost.

### Customising foundation models

- **Prompt engineering** with task-specific templates comes first.
- **Bedrock fine-tuning** with labelled JSONL, **continued pre-training** with unlabelled text, and **distillation** from a teacher model. Custom models are served with provisioned throughput or on-demand, depending on the model.
- **SageMaker JumpStart** and Hugging Face containers for full fine-tuning or LoRA on your own instances.
- **Optimising retrieval**: pick a better embedding model, tune chunk size and overlap, add reranking and hybrid search, and filter on metadata.

### Self-check

1. Churn predictions must be explained per customer. Which model and tool?
2. Training loss falls while validation loss rises after epoch 8. What do you do?
3. Cheapest way to tune four hyperparameters?
4. Loss jumps around and never converges. What do you change first?
5. Fine-tuning made the model forget general skills. What is this called, and name one fix.

**Answers.** 1: XGBoost explained with Clarify SHAP values. 2: Early stopping or more regularisation. 3: Bayesian AMT with early stopping (and spot instances). 4: Lower the learning rate or raise the batch size. 5: Catastrophic forgetting; mix in general data or use LoRA-style adapters with a lower learning rate.
