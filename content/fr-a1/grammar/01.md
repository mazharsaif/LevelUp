# Pronunciation, accents and the alphabet
intro: French spelling looks hard because many letters are silent, but the rules are regular: once you know them you can read almost any word aloud. In the DELF A1 speaking test you may have to spell your name, so learn the letter names, especially the ones that differ from English.

## Part: Letter names: the trap letters
Most French letter names are close to English. These are the ones that catch English and Hindi speakers when they spell a name:

| Letter | Say | Hindi | Careful |
|---|---|---|---|
| **E** | uh | {hi:अ} | not "ee" |
| **I** | ee | {hi:ई} | not "eye" |
| **G** | zhay | {hi:ज़्हे} | soft, like the s in "measure" |
| **J** | zhee | {hi:ज़्ही} | not "jay" |
| **U** | ü (lips round, say "ee") | {hi:यू} | not "you" |
| **R** | ehr | {hi:ऐर} | soft throat r |
| **H** | ahsh | {hi:आश} | |
| **Y** | ee-grek | {hi:ई-ग्रेक} | "Greek i" |
| **W** | doo-bluh-vay | {hi:दू-ब्ल-वे} | "double v" |

## Part: Accents
Accents are part of the spelling. They show how to say a vowel, or tell two words apart.

| Mark | Name | Sound | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| é | accent aigu | **ay** (closed e) | {say:le café}, {say:l'été} |
| è / ê | accent grave / circonflexe | **eh** (open e) | {say:la mère}, {say:la fête} |
| à, ù | accent grave | no change, tells words apart | à (to) / a (has), où (where) / ou (or) |
| â, î, ô, û | circonflexe | almost no change | le gâteau, l'île, l'hôtel |
| ç | cédille | **s** before a, o, u | {say:le français}, {say:ça} |
| ë, ï | tréma | say both vowels | Noël (noh-EL) |
> tip: Capital letters keep their accents in good French (École, À bientôt). If you type without accents in the app, your answer is accepted with a reminder.
## Part: Vowel sounds
Two or three vowels together often make **one** sound.

| Spelling | Sound | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| ou | oo | {say:vous}, {say:rouge} |
| u | ü: say "ee" with round lips | {say:tu}, {say:la rue} |
| oi | wah | {say:moi}, {say:trois} |
| ai, ei | eh | {say:le lait}, {say:la neige} |
| au, eau | oh | {say:au revoir}, {say:le bateau} |
| eu, œu | uh | {say:deux}, {say:la sœur} |
| e at the end of a word | silent | la table, une porte |
> warn: **u** and **ou** change the meaning: tu (you) ≠ tout (all), la rue (street) ≠ la roue (wheel).
## Part: Nasal vowels
A vowel + **n** or **m** at the end of a syllable is said through the nose, and the n / m itself is not said. Our hints write them **ahñ**, **ohñ**, **añ**.

| Spelling | Hint | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| an, am, en, em | ahñ | {say:la France}, {say:le temps}, {say:l'enfant} |
| on, om | ohñ | {say:bonjour}, {say:le nom} |
| in, im, ain, ein, un | añ | {say:le vin}, {say:le pain}, {say:un} |
| ien | yañ | {say:bien}, {say:indien} |
> tip: When a vowel follows the n, it is not nasal: bon (BOHÑ) but bonne (BUN), un (AÑ) but une (ÜN).
## Part: Consonants and silent letters
| Spelling | Sound | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| final d, p, s, t, x, z | usually silent | {say:le lit}, {say:trois}, {say:le nez} |
| final c, r, f, l | often said ("CaReFuL") | le sac, la mer, neuf, le sel |
| -er at the end of a verb | ay (r silent) | {say:parler}, {say:manger} |
| -ent at the end of a verb | silent | ils parlent = eel PARL |
| h | always silent | {say:l'hôtel}, {say:l'homme} |
| ch | sh | {say:le chat}, {say:la chambre} |
| j, g before e / i | zh | {say:je}, {say:la page} |
| gn | ny | {say:la montagne}, {say:l'Espagne} |
| qu | k | {say:qui}, {say:quatre} |
| ill (after a vowel: -aille, -eille) | y | {say:la fille}, {say:la bouteille} |
| r | soft throat r, never rolled | {say:Paris}, {say:la rue} |
## Part: Linking words: elision, liaison and stress
- **Elision:** le, la, je, ne, de, que lose their vowel before a vowel or silent h: l'école, j'habite, je n'aime pas, qu'il.
- **Liaison:** a silent final consonant is said when the next word starts with a vowel: les‿amis (lay-zah-MEE), vous‿avez (voo-zah-VAY), un‿enfant (uñ-nahñ-FAHÑ).
- **Stress:** French words are said evenly, with a little more weight on the **last** syllable: ba-NANE, ho-TEL. Our hints write that syllable in CAPITALS.

---mistakes
- Spelling E as "ee" and I as "eye" | E = "uh", I = "ee" | French letter names: see the first table.
- Saying the s of trois or the t of petit | trwah, puh-TEE | Final d, p, s, t, x, z are usually silent.
- Saying the -ent of ils parlent | eel PARL | The verb ending -ent is silent.
- Saying the h of hôtel | oh-TEL | The h is always silent in French.
- tu said like "too" | tu = tü (round lips, say "ee") | tout is "too"; tu is a different vowel.
