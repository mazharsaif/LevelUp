3. Pros, cons and recommendation

A. Box at top of Words

Pros
  • Where Batul asked for it (Words tab), no new tab (the helper design rule: no extra tab).
  • Result stays in the page: no sheet, keyboard does not hide it.
  • Collapsed it costs one line; the word list stays one tap away.
  • Local lookup and the word list sit next to each other, so "in your lessons" feels natural.
Cons
  • Pushes the list down when open (about half a screen).
  • Two inputs on one page (search and translate) may confuse at first: mitigated by clear labels and the collapsed state.
Recommended. Effort M.

B. Floating button + sheet

Pros
  • Always reachable; list untouched.
  • Same pattern as the study helper and word cards (consistent).
  • Could later appear on other tabs (Path, lessons) with one component.
Cons
  • Keyboard covers half the sheet on a phone, so long results scroll.
  • Button covers the last row of the list and can clash with the Ask chips.
  • Easy to confuse with the study helper.
  • History is cramped in a sheet.
Good second choice, esp. if you want it on every tab.

C. Sub-tab "Translate" in Words

Pros
  • Full page: room for history and bigger results.
  • No clutter in the word list; clear mental model ("a section", Batul's wording).
Cons
  • Hides the list and its search while translating; cannot compare with it.
  • One more tap before first use; easy to miss.
  • Adds a page to maintain (state, scroll, back button).
Choose if the box gets heavy over time.

Recommendation: Option A. It matches the approved plan (box on Words, local lookup first, 200 characters, same backend), costs the least to build, and the collapsed one-liner keeps the list as the main thing. If, after use, learners find the box too heavy, C is a small step from A (the panel is the same component).

DecisionProposed default
Local lookup firstSearch the 821 words and 208 phrases (accent-insensitive), exact match first
AI/api/ask with a translate mode, 200 chars, cache by text, same quota
AudioOnly for words from the lessons (pre-generated clips). No audio for typed text.
Add to my wordsOnly for lesson words (marks them learned/known as the card does). AI-only words: "Add" is hidden in v1 (no card, no audio); option to save to a personal list later.
CoursesGerman and French first (labels follow the course); others hidden
Signed-in onlyLocal lookup works for anyone; AI needs a login (as the helper)

4. How it relates to the study helper

  • Same backend, different job. The helper answers questions about the open card and refuses anything else. The translator only translates and explains the words; it never chats. Separate system prompt, same Groq key, cache, rate limit, daily cap and logging.
  • Different entry. The helper stays inside lessons and cards (chips under the card). The translator lives on Words, outside lessons, so it does not give away answers (the helper rule).
  • Bridge between them. In a sentence result, "Ask the helper about this" opens the helper sheet with the sentence as the topic, so a learner who wants "why" gets a short explained answer without a chat tab. Tapping a word chip gives a one-line explanation (one extra cheap AI call, cached).
  • Shared safeguards. One quota for both (1 per 2 s, 30 per hour), the "answers can be wrong" note, and the flag button for reports.
  • Open question for Mazhar: should "Add to my words" exist for AI-only words (needs a personal list), or lesson words only in v1? The designs assume lesson words only.
